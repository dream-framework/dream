T1 — Topological Quantization Confirmed

Integer-valued topological invariants on the compact Meta-Manifold label discrete 4D classes; charges and indices remain quantized after projection.

\[ Q_{\mathrm{U(1)}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma_2}F\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad k=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int \operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)\in\mathbb{Z}. \]

Operational. Discrete charges and instantons; selection rules; anomaly cancellation.

T2 — Symmetry Projection Partial

If the kernel is equivariant, Noether currents commute with projection: continuous 10D symmetries yield conserved 4D quantities.

\[ K_\lambda(gx;gX)=K_\lambda(x;X)\ \Rightarrow\ \partial_\mu J^\mu=0. \]

Operational. Test conservation to high precision; search for symmetry-breaking artifacts.

T3 — Coherence Threshold (Retention Law) Confirmed

A coherence scale \(\lambda_q\) separates regimes: below, interference-grade invariants persist; above, coarse-graining dominates.

\[ R(\lambda)=\exp[-(\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_q})^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}],\quad y=\ln(-\ln R)=D_{\mathrm{eff}}\ln\lambda-D_{\mathrm{eff}}\ln\lambda_q. \]

Operational. Two channels with shared \(\lambda_q\): HF power \(R_{\mathrm{HF}}(\lambda)\) and structural correlation \(R_{\mathrm{corr}^2}(\lambda)\).

T4 — Structure Focusing & CPI Partial

Small fiber Jacobians produce hubs/filaments persisting under smoothing; CPI quantifies local microstate budgets and predicts where structure survives longer.

\[ A(x)\sim\langle J_F^{-1}\rangle,\quad M(\lambda,\varepsilon;x)\sim A(x)(\tfrac{\lambda}{\varepsilon})^{D_I},\quad \mathrm{CPI}=\log_{10} M. \]

Operational. Elevated retention in high-\(A(x)\) regions; slower decay of structure vs HF detail.

T5 — Dimensional Flow to Homogeneity Partial

Under retention, effective dimension flows toward 3 on large scales: fractal signatures are finite-range only; homogeneity emerges above the cliff.

\[ D_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda)\nearrow 3\quad \text{as }\lambda\to\infty. \]

Operational. Counts approach cubic scaling beyond the cliff; cross-scale correlations vanish.

T6 — Cross-domain Ranked-Spectrum Ratio Law Partial

Cumulative retained modes scale as \(N(\Omega)\sim\Omega^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}\); ranked energies/masses obey ratio laws (base context-specific, ratios invariant).

\[ N(\Omega)\propto\Omega^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}},\quad \rho(\Omega)\propto\Omega^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}-1},\quad \frac{m_n}{m_1}=n^{1/D_{\mathrm{eff}}}. \]

Operational. Fit \(D_{\mathrm{eff}}\) from retained-mode counts/spectral slopes; check ranked-ratio predictions where ladders apply.

T7 — Interference (Aggregate Superposition of Local S2) Partially Supported

Under the non-invertible 10D → 4D projection, every aggregate 4D measurement is a superposition of local S2-governed retention processes. Pure S2 emerges only when the measurement isolates a sufficiently narrow component of the underlying retention distribution. Aggregate measurements produce probe-scale effective parameters rather than universal system-level \(D\) values.

\[ R_{\mathrm{obs}}(\lambda)=\int\rho(x',t',D',\lambda_q')\,\exp\!\left[-\left(\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_q'}\right)^{D'}\right]dx'\,dt'\,dD'\,d\lambda_q'. \]

Derivation from axioms and theorems

  1. A2 (Finite-resolution projection): The kernel \(K_\lambda\) is non-invertible — multiple distinct 10D Meta-Manifold configurations project to the same 4D observables. The projection is not one-to-one.
  2. A2 (Finite-resolution, continued): The kernel has finite resolution; it cannot distinguish sub-pixel MM structures. Any 4D measurement samples a finite volume and integrates over many MM localities.
  3. T3 (Retention Law, applied locally): Each locality \((x', t')\) has its own retention process governed by S2 with local parameters \((D', \lambda_q')\). The local topology of the MM varies, so these parameters vary across localities.
  4. T4 (Structure Focusing): The fiber Jacobian \(A(x)\) modulates microstate density, producing variable weighting \(\rho(x', t', D', \lambda_q')\) across the locality distribution.
  5. Conclusion (forced): The aggregate observed retention function is the integral of local S2 processes weighted by the locality density. Interference of S2s is unavoidable — it is a logical consequence of A2 + T3 + T4, not an optional add-on. T7 introduces no new postulate; it is a derived consequence. The fact that the framework generates additional theorems without growing its foundations is part of the claimed explanatory economy.
\[ D_{\mathrm{eff}}=D_{\mathrm{eff}}(\text{system},\,\text{probe scale})=F[\rho,\,K_\lambda,\,W]. \]

Falsifiable predictions

  1. Scale-dependent \(D_{\mathrm{eff}}\): As probe scale changes, measured \(D\) varies as different components dominate.
  2. Multi-component fits: As measurement resolution improves, single-S2 fits give way to multi-component fits.
  3. No universal \(D\): Systems are not characterized by a single \(D\); they are characterized by a distribution of local \(D\) values.
  4. Meta-S2 as exploratory: The distribution of measured \(D\) values is a distribution of effective parameters, not fundamental invariants.

Empirical status

Partially Supported — theory + empirics combined. The evidence chain is:

\[ \underbrace{\text{Axioms}}_{\text{theoretical}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{kernel composition + multiplicative survival}}_{\text{derivation}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{S2}}_{\text{predicted form}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{projection predicts interference}}_{\text{T7}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{aggregate observations}}_{\text{tested}} \]

This is not "we tried several curves and S2 happened to fit best." S2 is the theoretically selected solution to the assumed composition structure. The empirical question is whether observations are consistent with this prediction — and they are:

  • S2 ancestry survives out-of-sample testing. S2-mixture predicts held-out real data better than BIEXP and lognormal alternatives (85-88% win rate). These alternatives were disfavored by OOS comparison.
  • Single-S2 closure fails (as T7 predicts). 45-trial aggregation test confirmed that mixtures of S2s do NOT collapse to single S2. This strengthens the theory — interference is exactly what the non-invertible projection predicts.
  • INTERFERENCE + S2-present are not contradictory. Under T7, a dataset can simultaneously be INTERFERENCE (single-S2 insufficient) and S2-present (S2-family structure detectable). This is the expected outcome of projecting multiple local S2 processes.
  • Power-law remains empirically confounded. S2 is not yet uniquely distinguished from power-law ancestry in the tested regime. However, S2 has theoretical backing (derived from composition law) that power-law lacks (not derived from the present axioms). Observational degeneracy between two functions over a finite range does not make them theoretically equivalent — different underlying structures can become degenerate after compression.

Honest claim: The DREAM axioms derive S2 from a deformed kernel-composition law. Empirically, S2-family structure persists across heterogeneous aggregate observations, although single-S2 closure generally fails because of interference. Out-of-sample testing disfavors BIEXP and lognormal, while power-law remains empirically confounded with S2. The evidence is consistent with S2 as the underlying retention law, but does not yet uniquely identify S2 against all observationally degenerate alternatives.

Methodological lessons

1. High \(R^2\) alone is insufficient. Model selection must examine residual structure and penalized likelihood (AICc). The difference between \(R^2=0.997\) and \(R^2=0.9999\) looks trivial, while \(\Delta\mathrm{AICc}=744\) says the models are dramatically different.

2. In-sample AICc cannot distinguish ancestry from flexibility. S2_DUST (6 params) wins in-sample on 85% of synthetic non-S2 data. Out-of-sample prediction is required to separate genuine structural match from fitting flexibility.

3. Theory + empirics together are stronger than either alone. S2 is theoretically derived from the composition law. Empirically, it survives OOS testing against BIEXP and lognormal. Power-law is empirically confounded but lacks theoretical derivation. Observational degeneracy over a finite range does not make two functions theoretically equivalent — different underlying structures can become degenerate after compression.

The emerging picture

The combined theoretical and empirical evidence paints a coherent picture:

\[ \underbrace{\text{Local S2}}_{\text{derived from axioms}} \;\xrightarrow{\text{projection}}\; \underbrace{\text{Interference mixture}}_{\text{predicted by T7}} \;\xrightarrow{\text{observed}}\; \underbrace{\text{S2-family structure survives}}_{\text{confirmed by OOS}} \]

S2 is the local composition law. What we observe after projection is a superposition of processes generated by that law. Single-S2 closure fails (as T7 predicts), but S2-family ancestry survives out-of-sample testing. A dataset can simultaneously be INTERFERENCE (single-S2 insufficient) and S2-present (S2-family structure detectable) — these are not contradictory verdicts. Under T7, INTERFERENCE + S2 presence is the expected outcome.

Operational. Test for scale-dependent \(D_{\mathrm{eff}}\) with proper null-model controls; search for multi-component structure at varying measurement resolutions; treat fitted \(D\) as a probe-scale effective parameter, not a system invariant; always use AICc (not \(R^2\)) for model comparison on retention curves.

T7.1 — Multi-Regime S2 (Piecewise Composition) Confirmed

S2 is the local law of retention. The global retention curve is a piecewise composition of S2 regimes, with the kernel entering different regimes at reproducible fractional scale thresholds. Each regime is a clean S2; the transition scale \(\lambda^\*\) clusters at fractional positions \([0.25, 0.38]\) of the t-range (IQR) across unrelated domains.

\[ R(\lambda)\;\approx\; \begin{cases} \exp\!\Big[-\Big(\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{q,1}}\Big)^{D_1}\Big] & \lambda<\lambda^\*\\ A_2\,\exp\!\Big[-\Big(\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{q,2}}\Big)^{D_2}\Big] & \lambda\ge\lambda^\* \end{cases} \]

Intuition (layman)

Imagine a long road trip. Same car, same driver, same road — but the speed limit changes at every town boundary. Your GPS log shows piecewise behavior: 70 mph on the highway, 30 mph through town. The journey is one continuous drive, but the speed log has sharp transitions at reproducible milestones.

DREAM's retention law behaves the same way. S2 fits any single stretch of the curve. Across the full trajectory, the kernel enters different regimes — same mechanism, different effective parameters — at reproducible fractional positions. The kernel doesn't change; the information state underneath it does.

Derivation (refinement of T7)

  1. T3 (S2 local law): At any single locality \((x', t')\), retention is \(R=e^{-(\lambda/\lambda_q)^D}\) with local parameters.
  2. A2 (non-invertible projection): The kernel has finite resolution — it cannot resolve sub-λ structure. As the probe scale sweeps, the kernel samples different parts of the underlying information state.
  3. A6 (finite resolution ⇒ regimes): A coherence cliff at \(\lambda_q\) separates two regimes (below: interference-grade invariants retain; above: classical coarse-grain dominates). The kernel's effective parameters differ on either side.
  4. Conclusion (refinement): When the information state itself changes character across the probe range (e.g., from slowly-varying to rapidly-varying structure), the kernel enters a new S2 regime at a characteristic transition scale \(\lambda^\*\). The global curve is a piecewise composition of S2 regimes. T7.1 introduces no new postulate — it is the natural piecewise refinement of T7.

Falsifiable predictions

  1. P1: Piecewise S2 strongly beats single-S2 on real curves where the kernel crosses a regime boundary (ΔAICc > 4).
  2. P2: Transition positions cluster at reproducible fractional positions across unrelated domains (std < 0.20 in fractional coordinates).
  3. P3: D systematically increases from regime 1 to regime 2 — the kernel enters a regime where information is more susceptible to compression (D₂ > D₁ in >80% of cases).
  4. P4: Null-model control: pure single-S2 synthetic curves should NOT show this strong-improvement pattern above chance (binomial p < 0.001).
  5. P5: Positive control: the piecewise pipeline should recover known 2-regime t_break in >80% of synthetic 2-regime cases.

Why this is not "multiple Meta-Manifolds"

T7.1 was originally proposed as a multi-Meta-Manifold hypothesis: the 4D world as a superposition of multiple projected structures. That strong hypothesis is not supported:

  • Probe A (drift vs mixture): 10/10 real curves classified as DRIFT, 0/10 as discrete mixture. The closure violation is consistent with a single complicated kernel whose parameters drift — not with multiple discrete kernels.
  • Probe B (cross-domain residual correlation): 18/30 significant pairs (vs 1.5 expected by chance) — but no characteristic interference lag. Excess correlation is contemporaneous, not at a hidden interference scale.

This is exactly the identifiability wall (A2): we cannot prove "one Meta-Manifold" directly. But where we can discriminate, the data is consistent with one complicated kernel, not with multiple independent kernels.

Honest caveats

  • 24% of datasets show D₂ hitting the upper bound (D=10), suggesting the "second regime" might not be S2 but a different decay law (e.g., super-Gaussian) that the S2 form approximates.
  • Clustering magnitude is modest: real std (0.169) is only 1.05× tighter than single-S2 null (0.178). Statistical significance is huge; effect size is small.
  • Sample size is 25 real datasets. More would strengthen the claim, but the existing evidence is decisive against the single-S2 null.
  • D₁ and D₂ are not correlated (Spearman ρ=0.019, p=0.93). The two regimes appear to be independently drawn, not constrained to a 1D manifold in (D₁, D₂) space. This is consistent with "the kernel enters a new regime" but argues against "the second regime is a deterministic function of the first."

Operational. When fitting S2 to a retention curve, always test piecewise-S2 with a searched breakpoint as an alternative. Report ΔAICc, the optimal t_break fraction, and the regime pair (D₁, D₂). Flag entries where D₂ hits the upper bound — these may need a different second-regime functional form.
