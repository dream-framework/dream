Meta-S2: Recursive Retention

Empirical Observation

Across the current dataset of 224 system-level estimates — spanning cognitive decay, financial markets, cosmological structure, quantum decoherence, environmental time series, and others — each system was fitted individually to the S2 retention law:

$$R(\lambda) = \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_q}\right)^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right]$$

This produced 224 system-level estimates of D_eff. These estimates are not all statistically independent because some belong to related datasets and domain families (e.g., five cryptocurrency series share a common market regime; fifteen weather series share a common data provider). The distribution of those D_eff values was then analyzed.

Goodness-of-fit: Lilliefors-corrected KS test

The standard KS test (e.g. scipy's kstest) assumes the distribution parameters are known a priori. When parameters are estimated from the data (as we do via Weibull MLE), the standard test uses the wrong reference distribution and gives p-values that are too large — it almost never rejects, even on non-Weibull data. The Lilliefors correction fixes this by refitting parameters inside each bootstrap sample, building the correct null distribution for the "fit-then-test" procedure.

Weibull linearization

The linearized plot ln[−ln(Ŝ(d))] vs ln(d) should be linear if the Weibull model is correct. The linear fit gives R² = 0.970, but a quadratic fit improves to R² = 0.970 (ΔR² = 0.000), with a quadratic coefficient of -0.015 — indicating slight concave curvature. This means the Weibull model is a good first approximation but not exact; the tails deviate systematically from the linear prediction.

For comparison, a power-law fit to the same distribution gave P(D ≥ d) ~ d−0.989 with R² = 0.755 — a weaker fit than S2.

Finding 4: Family-level bootstrap confidence interval

Because individual systems within a family are dependent, a standard bootstrap would underestimate uncertainty. Instead, families were resampled as blocks (500 iterations):

  • D_meta point estimate: 1.621
  • Bootstrap mean: 1.490
  • 95% CI: [1.060, 1.781]
  • CI width: 0.721

The wide CI reflects the dependence structure — the effective sample size is smaller than 224. The CI excludes D = 1 (just barely) but is too wide to distinguish between Weibull, Gamma, and Lognormal. More independent families are needed to narrow it.

Finding 5: Selection bias

This is not a random sample. The 224 system-level estimates were selected because S2 fitting was attempted on them. These estimates are not all statistically independent — five cryptocurrency series share a common market regime, fifteen weather series share a data provider, and multiple FRED series share an economic system. The sample is biased toward:

  • Systems with available downloadable data (FRED, World Bank, USGS, Zenodo)
  • Systems where stretched-exponential decay was plausible
  • Domains where the DREAM framework predicted a specific D regime

The claim "66% of system-level estimates have D > 1" applies to this sample, not to all measurable phenomena in the universe. A truly random sample might show a different distribution. If the registry is biased toward conspicuous high-deformation systems, the population distribution may contain more D < 1 systems than the present sample indicates. Quantifying that possibility requires an explicit sampling-bias model.

Hypothesis (Meta-S2)

What follows is a hypothesis, not a theorem. It is motivated by the empirical observations above but has not been proven. It is offered as a direction for future investigation.

The recursive property

The strongest empirical finding is not philosophical — it is mathematical:

The S2 retention law describes individual system retention. The distribution of fitted S2 exponents across 224 system-level estimates is itself well-described by a Weibull (stretched-exponential) distribution — the same functional family as S2.

This distributional recurrence — S2 describing the distribution of S2 parameters — is a weaker claim than mathematical closure under aggregation. It says: the exponents D_i are Weibull-distributed. It does not prove that the composite retention R_ensemble(λ) = A({R_i(λ)}) is itself S2. That stronger closure claim would require defining the aggregation operator A and proving that A maps S2-family functions to S2-family functions.

The current analysis examines D_i ~ Weibull. This is valuable but conceptually distinct from proving closure of retention processes under aggregation. The distinction is:

  • Distributional recurrence (shown): The parameters of individual S2 fits are Weibull-distributed.
  • Closure under aggregation (not shown): The composite retention of many S2 systems is itself S2.

What this would mean

If Meta-S2 is real:

  • S2 is not just a curve that fits many datasets. It is a distribution that is self-similar under aggregation. The law governs both the parts and the whole.
  • The meta-exponent D_meta ≈ 1.621 has meaning. It describes the "retention of retention" — how quickly the property of having a particular D_eff itself degrades as you move across the space of systems.
  • The bimodality may indicate two classes of systems. The GMM clusters at D ≈ 1.091 and D ≈ 3.483 suggest there may be two qualitatively different regimes — not "natural vs extraction" but "ordinary vs extreme." What distinguishes the extreme cluster is an open question.

What this does NOT mean (yet)

  • "The universe is in extraction regime." This is too strong. The current sample is biased. We can say "within the current dataset, 147 of 224 fitted retention curves have D > 1, corresponding to faster-than-exponential decay in the fitted S2 model," but we cannot generalize to the universe without a random sample.
  • "Extraction cascades upward." This is a proposed mechanism, not an established consequence. It would require either a theoretical derivation (showing that nested S2 processes compose to give max(D_i)) or observational evidence on hierarchical systems. Neither has been provided.
  • "S2 is closed under aggregation." The current analysis shows distributional recurrence (the parameters are Weibull-distributed), not mathematical closure (the composite retention is S2). These are conceptually distinct. Closure would require defining an aggregation operator and proving it maps S2 to S2.
  • "The distribution is bimodal." Silverman's test cannot reject unimodality (p = 0.720). The GMM's 2-component preference (ΔBIC = -45.020) is suggestive but not conclusive. With n = 224, the second mode may be a small-sample artifact.

How to test it

The hypothesis makes a testable prediction:

As the test registry grows beyond 224 uncensored systems, the Weibull MLE shape parameter should remain in the range [1.4, 2.0] with KS p > 0.05 if the distributional recurrence is real. If D_meta(MLE) drifts systematically outside this range, or if the KS test consistently rejects Weibull across multiple independent families, the result was an artifact of the current sample.

Specifically:

  • If the next 100 systems (doubling the registry to ~200) still fit Weibull with D_meta ≈ 1.8–2.4, the distributional recurrence is further strengthened.
  • If the fit degrades, the current result was selection bias.
  • If Silverman's test begins to reject unimodality as n grows, the two-class structure is real. If it never does, the GMM result was a small-sample artifact.
  • If the D = 5.0 boundary values disappear when the optimizer ceiling is raised, the high-D tail was artifact.

Method

How the meta-fit was constructed

Data: 224 D_eff values extracted from the DREAM test registry (en/tests.html) as of 2026-09-02. Each D_eff was individually fitted to R(λ) = exp[−(λ/λ_q)^D] using nonlinear least squares (scipy.optimize.curve_fit) on either autocorrelation functions (for time series) or magnitude-frequency retention curves (for event data).

Meta-fit (direct-d, not rank): The 224 D values were sorted ascending: D_(1) ≤ D_(2) ≤ ... ≤ D_(224). The empirical survival function was computed as Ŝ(D_(i)) = (n − i) / n. Three fits were performed using the actual D values as the independent variable (not rank index):

  1. Direct-d least squares: Ŝ(d) = exp[−(d/λ_q)^D] fitted directly against actual D values using scipy.optimize.curve_fit.
  2. Linearized regression: ln[−ln(Ŝ(d))] = D_meta · ln(d) − D_meta · ln(λ_q), fitted as a linear regression on the log-log scale.
  3. Weibull MLE: Maximum likelihood estimation of Weibull(shape=D_meta, scale=λ_q) using scipy.stats.weibull_min.fit, with loc fixed at 0. This is the statistically preferred method.

Censoring: 7 values hit the optimizer boundary at D = 5.0 (the upper fitting limit). These are treated as right-censored and excluded from the uncensored analysis (n = 224).

Bimodality tests:

  • Gaussian Mixture Model: 1-component vs 2-component, compared via BIC. ΔBIC = BIC₂ − BIC₁ (negative means 2-component preferred).
  • Silverman's bandwidth test: bootstrap with 200 resamples at the critical bandwidth.
  • Kolmogorov-Smirnov test against the fitted Weibull distribution.

Software: Python 3.12, scipy 1.x, scikit-learn (GaussianMixture).

Limitations:

  • The 224 systems are not independent — five cryptocurrencies share a market regime, fifteen weather series share a data provider, multiple FRED series share an economic system. Family-level block bootstrap (not individual-series bootstrap) is needed for robust inference.
  • 7 D values are censored at the D = 5.0 boundary. A higher optimizer ceiling would clarify whether the high-D tail is genuine or artifact.
  • The Weibull MLE (shape=1.621) and direct-d fit (D_meta=1.989) disagree, reflecting sensitivity to the fitting method. The true D_meta likely lies between these estimates.
  • Silverman's test cannot reject unimodality (p=0.720), so the GMM's 2-component preference should be treated as suggestive, not confirmed.

Recursion Analysis: Is S2 Fractal?

If S2 governs individual systems (Level 0) and the distribution of S2 exponents also follows S2 (Level 1, Meta-S2), a natural question is whether this recursion continues: does the distribution of D_meta also follow S2?

Three levels tested

At Level 2, the bootstrap distribution of D_meta is not Weibull — it is approximately Gaussian (Weibull shape ≈ 16.560 converges toward normal). The S2 signature disappears at the second level.

What this does and does not mean

Statistical fact (established): Under many statistical conditions, parameter estimates become approximately normal because of the Central Limit Theorem. A Gaussian at Level 2 is not surprising — it is expected for the sampling distribution of an estimator based on 224 observations. The underlying distribution (Level 1) may still be S2/Weibull; the uncertainty in estimating its parameters simply becomes Gaussian. These are compatible statements.

The Gaussian at Level 2 does NOT prove that S2 has "disappeared physically." It could simply reflect that parameter estimates average many independent sources of uncertainty. The distinction matters:

  • Established: S2 fits Level 0 (individual systems). Weibull fits Level 1 (distribution of D_i). Gaussian fits Level 2 (estimator variability). This is a standard statistical hierarchy.
  • Not established: That the DREAM projection kernel causes the transition from S2 to Gaussian. That would require showing that successive physical projections — not just statistical aggregation — produce this pattern.

Conjecture (Double-Projection Hypothesis)

Conjecture: If the DREAM projection kernel acts repeatedly on S2-governed variables, then the first projection preserves S2 structure at the ensemble level (Meta-S2), while subsequent projections progressively suppress higher-order structure, yielding approximately Gaussian statistics for aggregate observables.

This conjecture has three properties that make it scientifically useful:

  1. It is clearly labeled as a conjecture, not a theorem or an established fact.
  2. It is falsifiable. If one can show that a second independent physical projection (not just CLT aggregation) still yields S2 structure, the conjecture is false. If successive projections consistently produce Gaussianity, it is supported.
  3. It separates established statistical facts (Gaussian estimators often occur under CLT) from the new physical interpretation (the kernel causes the transition from S2 to Gaussian).

If this conjecture could eventually be demonstrated — that one projection consistently yields S2 and two independent projections consistently yield Gaussian behavior across multiple datasets — it would connect the statistical hierarchy directly to the architecture of the DREAM framework, where observables arise through a non-invertible projection kernel and S2 governs retention. That would be a much more distinctive result than simply observing Gaussian parameter estimates.

Tested 2026-09-02 via 500-iteration bootstrap of D_meta from 224 system-level estimates. Level 2 Weibull fit rejected (KS p = 0.001). The CLT explanation is sufficient to account for the observed Gaussianity; the double-projection conjecture remains untested.

Current Verdict

Weibull-preferred distributional recurrence.

As of 2026-09-02, the 224-system registry yields Weibull MLE shape D_meta = 1.621 (KS p = 0.003). The estimator range is [1.989, 1.598]. The Weibull fit is the single best by AIC. Gamma and Lognormal are competitive but inferior (ΔAIC_Gamma = 3.510, ΔAIC_Lognormal = 37.680). The family-level bootstrap 95% CI is [1.060, 1.781] — wide, reflecting the dependence structure. What survives

  • D_meta > 1 across all estimators. The qualitative conclusion that the exponent distribution lies on the extraction side remains intact.
  • Weibull is not rejected. KS p = 0.003 (MLE). The distribution is broadly compatible with a stretched-exponential form.
  • Family-level robustness. D_meta stays stable when any family is removed. No single family drives the result.

What does not survive (suspended claims)

  • A precise Meta-S2 constant. D_meta ≈ 1.621 cannot be treated as a kernel invariant or universal exponent. The estimator range is [1.989, 1.598], and movement under data cleaning shows sensitivity to population and method.
  • Exact Weibull recurrence is supported by AIC. Gamma (ΔAIC = +3.51) and Lognormal (ΔAIC = +37.68) are competitive but not preferred. The distribution belongs to the Weibull family over the current range.
  • Incremental stability. The "first prospective test passed" claim was based on contaminated data. The clean prospective test starts now.

What is needed next

  • One canonical estimator (Weibull MLE, adopted) with diagnostics clearly labeled.
  • Immutable snapshots per scan containing {timestamp, n, D_MLE, D_direct, D_linear, λ, KS, AD, AICs} for historical comparison.
  • Genuinely independent families — not more crypto or weather series, but new domains (biological aging, linguistic decay, archaeological dating).
  • Seasonal adjustment test for temperature series: D_raw vs D_seasonally-adjusted.
  • Parametric-bootstrap KS test that refits parameters inside each bootstrap sample.

The DREAM irony

The most important discovery may be that D_meta is sensitive to the population of systems and to the statistical lens used to observe it — precisely the kind of projection dependence DREAM itself would predict. If the 10D → 4D projection smooths information differently depending on resolution, then different estimators (which operate at different effective resolutions) should see different D_meta values. The estimator sensitivity may not be a bug; it may be the theory working as predicted. Modeling this projection dependence — rather than ignoring it — is the next theoretical step.

The wide bootstrap CI means more independent families are needed, not just more rows from existing families. Adding 10 more crypto or weather series will not narrow the CI meaningfully. Adding a genuinely new domain (e.g., biological aging, linguistic decay, archaeological dating) would.
