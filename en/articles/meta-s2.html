Meta-S2: Recursive Retention

Empirical Observation

Across the current dataset of 91 system-level estimates — spanning cognitive decay, financial markets, cosmological structure, quantum decoherence, environmental time series, and others — each system was fitted individually to the S2 retention law:

$$R(\lambda) = \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_q}\right)^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right]$$

This produced 91 system-level estimates of D_eff. These estimates are not all statistically independent because some belong to related datasets and domain families (e.g., five cryptocurrency series share a common market regime; fifteen weather series share a common data provider). The distribution of those D_eff values was then analyzed.

Finding 1: The distribution of D values follows S2

The complementary cumulative distribution (survival function) of D_eff — that is, the fraction of systems with D ≥ d — was fitted directly against the actual D values to the S2 / Weibull functional form:

$$\hat{S}(d) = P(D \ge d) = \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{d}{\lambda_{\mathrm{meta}}}\right)^{D_{\mathrm{meta}}}\right]$$

Three fits were performed:

All three methods agree that the distribution of D_eff is well-described by a Weibull (stretched-exponential) survival function with D_meta ≈ 1.59 and λ_meta ≈ 1.45 in D units. The nonlinear direct-d fit and Weibull-linearized fit produce nearly identical shape estimates (1.588 vs 1.591). Their R² values are not directly comparable because they are calculated in different transformed spaces. A KS goodness-of-fit test did not reject the fitted Weibull model at the 5% level (p = 0.165). Note: because the Weibull parameters were estimated from the same 91 observations, the ordinary tabulated KS p-value is not strictly valid; a parametric-bootstrap KS test that refits parameters inside each bootstrap sample would be preferable.

Prospective update — July 28, 2026: After 16 additional registry tests increased the Meta-S2 sample from 75 to 91 eligible systems, D_meta changed from 1.577 to 1.588 (ΔD = 0.011, ~0.7%) and R² improved from 0.9865 to 0.9897. The first out-of-sample registry expansion therefore remained within the predicted stability interval. This is materially stronger evidence than the original static fit: the estimated shape parameter remained nearly unchanged during a meaningful registry expansion while the fit improved.

The Weibull fit is adequate but not uniquely preferred. The Anderson-Darling statistic (1.031) is above the 5% critical value (~0.757), suggesting the fit is marginal. By AIC, both Lognormal (ΔAIC = −3.4) and Gamma (ΔAIC = −4.0) are preferred over Weibull. All three are clearly superior to Exponential. Weibull linearization

The linearized plot ln[−ln(Ŝ(d))] vs ln(d) should be linear if the Weibull model is correct. The linear fit gives R² = 0.967, but a quadratic fit improves to R² = 0.988 (ΔR² = 0.021), with a quadratic coefficient of −0.239 — indicating slight concave curvature. This means the Weibull model is a good first approximation but not exact; the tails deviate systematically from the linear prediction.

For comparison, a power-law fit to the same distribution gave P(D ≥ d) ~ d−1.015 with R² = 0.755 — a weaker fit than S2.

Finding 4: Family-level bootstrap confidence interval

Because individual systems within a family are dependent, a standard bootstrap would underestimate uncertainty. Instead, families were resampled as blocks (500 iterations):

  • D_meta point estimate: 1.595
  • Bootstrap mean: 1.626
  • 95% CI: [1.253, 2.270]
  • CI width: 1.017

The wide CI (±0.5) reflects the dependence structure — the effective sample size is smaller than 95. The CI excludes D = 1 (just barely) but is too wide to distinguish between Weibull, Gamma, and Lognormal. More independent families are needed to narrow it.

Finding 5: Selection bias

This is not a random sample. The 91 system-level estimates were selected because S2 fitting was attempted on them. These estimates are not all statistically independent — five cryptocurrency series share a common market regime, fifteen weather series share a data provider, and multiple FRED series share an economic system. The sample is biased toward:

  • Systems with available downloadable data (FRED, World Bank, USGS, Zenodo)
  • Systems where stretched-exponential decay was plausible
  • Domains where the DREAM framework predicted a specific D regime

The claim "62% of system-level estimates have D > 1" applies to this sample, not to all measurable phenomena in the universe. A truly random sample might show a different distribution. If the registry is biased toward conspicuous high-deformation systems, the population distribution may contain more D < 1 systems than the present sample indicates. Quantifying that possibility requires an explicit sampling-bias model.

Hypothesis (Meta-S2)

What follows is a hypothesis, not a theorem. It is motivated by the empirical observations above but has not been proven. It is offered as a direction for future investigation.

The recursive property

The strongest empirical finding is not philosophical — it is mathematical:

The S2 retention law describes individual system retention. The distribution of fitted S2 exponents across 91 system-level estimates is itself well-described by a Weibull (stretched-exponential) distribution — the same functional family as S2.

This distributional recurrence — S2 describing the distribution of S2 parameters — is a weaker claim than mathematical closure under aggregation. It says: the exponents D_i are Weibull-distributed. It does not prove that the composite retention R_ensemble(λ) = A({R_i(λ)}) is itself S2. That stronger closure claim would require defining the aggregation operator A and proving that A maps S2-family functions to S2-family functions.

The current analysis examines D_i ~ Weibull. This is valuable but conceptually distinct from proving closure of retention processes under aggregation. The distinction is:

  • Distributional recurrence (shown): The parameters of individual S2 fits are Weibull-distributed.
  • Closure under aggregation (not shown): The composite retention of many S2 systems is itself S2.

What this would mean

If Meta-S2 is real:

  • S2 is not just a curve that fits many datasets. It is a distribution that is self-similar under aggregation. The law governs both the parts and the whole.
  • The meta-exponent D_meta ≈ 1.6 has meaning. It describes the "retention of retention" — how quickly the property of having a particular D_eff itself degrades as you move across the space of systems.
  • The bimodality may indicate two classes of systems. The GMM clusters at D ≈ 1.1 and D ≈ 4.5 suggest there may be two qualitatively different regimes — not "natural vs extraction" but "ordinary vs extreme." What distinguishes the extreme cluster is an open question.

What this does NOT mean (yet)

  • "The universe is in extraction regime." This is too strong. The current sample is biased. We can say "within the current dataset, 56 of 91 fitted retention curves have D > 1, corresponding to faster-than-exponential decay in the fitted S2 model," but we cannot generalize to the universe without a random sample.
  • "Extraction cascades upward." This is a proposed mechanism, not an established consequence. It would require either a theoretical derivation (showing that nested S2 processes compose to give max(D_i)) or observational evidence on hierarchical systems. Neither has been provided.
  • "S2 is closed under aggregation." The current analysis shows distributional recurrence (the parameters are Weibull-distributed), not mathematical closure (the composite retention is S2). These are conceptually distinct. Closure would require defining an aggregation operator and proving it maps S2 to S2.
  • "The distribution is bimodal." Silverman's test cannot reject unimodality (p = 0.46). The GMM's 2-component preference (ΔBIC = −12.3) is suggestive but not conclusive. With n = 71, the second mode may be a small-sample artifact.

How to test it

The hypothesis makes a testable prediction:

As the test registry grows beyond 91 systems, the Weibull fit to the distribution of D values should remain stable (D_meta ≈ 1.4–1.9, KS p > 0.05) if the distributional recurrence is real. If D_meta drifts significantly or the KS test rejects Weibull as more systems are added, the current result was an artifact of the sample.

First prospective test (July 28, 2026): PASSED. Registry expanded from 75 → 91 systems. D_meta changed from 1.577 to 1.588 (Δ = 0.7%). R² improved from 0.9865 to 0.9897. The prediction was fulfilled.

Specifically:

  • If the next 100 systems (doubling the registry to ~200) still fit Weibull with D_meta ≈ 1.4–1.9, the distributional recurrence is further strengthened.
  • If the fit degrades, the current result was selection bias.
  • If Silverman's test begins to reject unimodality as n grows, the two-class structure is real. If it never does, the GMM result was a small-sample artifact.
  • If the D = 5.0 boundary values disappear when the optimizer ceiling is raised, the high-D tail was artifact.

Method

How the meta-fit was constructed

Data: 91 D_eff values extracted from the DREAM test registry (en/tests.html) as of 2026-07-29. Each D_eff was individually fitted to R(λ) = exp[−(λ/λ_q)^D] using nonlinear least squares (scipy.optimize.curve_fit) on either autocorrelation functions (for time series) or magnitude-frequency retention curves (for event data).

Meta-fit (direct-d, not rank): The 91 D values were sorted ascending: D_(1) ≤ D_(2) ≤ ... ≤ D_(91). The empirical survival function was computed as Ŝ(D_(i)) = (n − i) / n. Three fits were performed using the actual D values as the independent variable (not rank index):

  1. Direct-d least squares: Ŝ(d) = exp[−(d/λ_q)^D] fitted directly against actual D values using scipy.optimize.curve_fit.
  2. Linearized regression: ln[−ln(Ŝ(d))] = D_meta · ln(d) − D_meta · ln(λ_q), fitted as a linear regression on the log-log scale.
  3. Weibull MLE: Maximum likelihood estimation of Weibull(shape=D_meta, scale=λ_q) using scipy.stats.weibull_min.fit, with loc fixed at 0. This is the statistically preferred method.

Censoring: Three values hit the optimizer boundary at D = 5.0 (the upper fitting limit). These are treated as right-censored and excluded from the uncensored analysis (n = 71).

Bimodality tests:

  • Gaussian Mixture Model: 1-component vs 2-component, compared via BIC. ΔBIC = BIC₂ − BIC₁ (negative means 2-component preferred).
  • Silverman's bandwidth test: bootstrap with 200 resamples at the critical bandwidth.
  • Kolmogorov-Smirnov test against the fitted Weibull distribution.

Software: Python 3.12, scipy 1.x, scikit-learn (GaussianMixture).

Limitations:

  • The 91 systems are not independent — five cryptocurrencies share a market regime, fifteen weather series share a data provider, multiple FRED series share an economic system. Family-level block bootstrap (not individual-series bootstrap) is needed for robust inference.
  • Three D values are censored at the D = 5.0 boundary. A higher optimizer ceiling would clarify whether the high-D tail is genuine or artifact.
  • The Weibull MLE (shape=1.40) and direct-d fit (D_meta=1.91) disagree by ~0.5, reflecting sensitivity to the fitting method. The true D_meta likely lies between these estimates.
  • Silverman's test cannot reject unimodality (p=0.46), so the GMM's 2-component preference should be treated as suggestive, not confirmed.

Current Verdict

Incrementally stable evidence for Meta-S2 distributional recurrence.

As the Meta-S2 sample increased from 75 to 91 systems, the estimated Weibull shape parameter remained stable near D_meta ≈ 1.59 (ΔD = 0.011, ~0.7%), while R² improved from 0.9865 to 0.9897. This is consistent with, but does not prove, the distributional recurrence hypothesis. The Weibull fit is adequate (KS p = 0.165) but not uniquely preferred (Gamma and Lognormal are competitive by AIC). The family-level bootstrap 95% CI is [1.25, 2.27] — wide, reflecting the dependence structure. The first prospective prediction (stability under registry expansion) has been fulfilled.

The next decisive milestone is not merely adding more rows. It is demonstrating that the estimate remains stable under:

  • Family-level exclusion — shown: D_meta stays in [1.45, 1.80] when any family is removed.
  • Clustered resampling — shown: family-block bootstrap gives CI [1.25, 2.27].
  • Preprocessing changes — not yet done: temperature series should be tested before and after seasonal adjustment. If D > 1 disappears after removing the annual cycle, the "extraction" interpretation for temperature is an artifact of deterministic seasonality, not structural information loss.
  • Alternative goodness-of-fit tests — shown: KS (p=0.165, cannot reject) and AD (1.031, marginal). Both are borderline. More data or more powerful tests are needed.

The wide bootstrap CI means more independent families are needed, not just more rows from existing families. Adding 10 more crypto or weather series will not narrow the CI meaningfully. Adding a genuinely new domain (e.g., biological aging, linguistic decay, archaeological dating) would.
