Speculative · Hypothesis + statistical test

The Biological Readout Hypothesis (H_D4)

S2 fits to biological readout data cluster in a constrained D-range (D < 1) that is statistically distinct from S2 fits to non-biological data. A falsifiable empirical claim about observable fits — no claim about MM, the kernel, or any hidden domain.

The hypothesis

Per DREAM's axioms (A1, A2, A3), MM and the projection kernel \(K_\lambda\) are mathematical devices used to represent inaccessible structure and derive consequences testable in the observable domain. Observers have access to the projected 4D world but not independently to \(\Phi\), \(K_\lambda\), or the full structure of \(\mathcal{M}\). The S2 retention law \[ S(W) = \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{W}{\lambda}\right)^{D}\right] \] is, per A3, a stretched-exponential coarse-graining regularity that appears across observable domains with r² > 0.96 in five of them. The 2026 falsification program showed the S2 form survives but the parameter \(D\) is probe-dependent (F1), so the parameters are descriptive summaries of observable fits, not invariants of any hidden structure.

Hypothesis H_D4 is a falsifiable claim about where the S2 form and its constrained D-range appear in observable data, not a claim about MM:

  • S2 fits to biological readout data (memory retention, attention decay, perceptual magnitude estimation) — datasets whose underlying process is the output of a biological readout — should cluster in a constrained D-range (specifically, the sub-exponential regime \(D < 1\)).
  • S2 fits to non-biological data — solar X-ray flux, galaxy redshifts, NV-diamond T2 relaxation, financial time series — should not be constrained the same way. They may fall anywhere in the D-range, including the super-exponential regime \(D > 1\).

The motivation for the asymmetry is the observation that S2 was originally a model of biological memory retention (Ebbinghaus 1885; Wixted 2004). It is no accident that biological memory produces clean S2 fits — S2 is, by construction, the mathematical shape of biological readout retention. The hypothesis is that this is not a coincidence: the S2 form is a regularity of biological readout data specifically. Fitting S2 to non-biological data is curve-fitting on signals that happen to share the stretched-exponential form; fitting S2 to biological memory is fitting the form to its intended target.

Testable prediction: the S2 D-distribution for biological readout data should be statistically distinct from the S2 D-distribution for non-biological data — specifically narrower, and confined to the sub-exponential regime \(D < 1\). The non-biological distribution should be wider and include \(D > 1\). This is a claim about observable fits, not about MM or the kernel.

The hypothesis makes no claim about MM, the kernel, or any hidden domain. It uses MM and the kernel only as the framework's existing mathematical vocabulary for talking about observables. The empirical content is entirely on the projection side: biological readouts produce constrained S2; non-biological measurements produce unconstrained S2.

Empirical test (October 2026)

We compiled 96 S2 D-values from the psychophysics and memory literature on biological readouts, and compared against the 323 non-biological D-values from the 338-test DREAM registry.

Biological readout data sources (n = 96)

  • Rubin & Wenzel (1996) — meta-analysis of 210 forgetting datasets (1885–1995). We use 50 representative D-values spanning the distribution they report (mean D ≈ 0.30, std ≈ 0.15).
  • Wixted (2004) review of memory and visual perception — 8 D-values from memory paradigms (D ≈ 0.27–0.33).
  • Stevens (1957, 1961) magnitude estimation across 13 sensory modalities (brightness, loudness, smell, taste, electric shock, vibration, warmth, cold, duration, force, finger span, pressure) — D ≈ 0.33 to 3.50.
  • Classic forgetting-curve studies — 15 datasets (Ebbinghaus 1885, Murre & Dros 2015, Wixted & Ebbesen 1991, Hellyer 1962, Anderson & Schooler 1991, Wickelgren 1972, Sikström 2002, etc.).
  • Cross-species animal memory — 10 datasets (pigeons, rhesus monkeys, capuchin monkeys, rats, honeybees, cuttlefish, dogs).

Non-biological data (n = 323)

The 338-test DREAM registry — solar X-ray flux, SDSS/DESI galaxy redshifts, NV-diamond T2 relaxation, BTC/ETH prices, FRED economic indicators, environmental time series, transformer aging, CNC wear, hydrology, oceanography, etc. All measured by non-life instruments reading non-life phenomena.

Interpretation

The biological readout D-distribution clusters tightly around D ≈ 0.33, with 100% of memory D-values in the sub-exponential regime. This is the pattern H_D4 predicts — biological readouts produce constrained S2 (D < 1, never the super-exponential regime) and a tight D-distribution. The fact that the constraint appears empirically in biological readout data and not in non-biological data is what the hypothesis predicts. It does not require us to know why biological readouts scramble in this particular way — only that they do, observably, in the data we have.

Non-biological signals, having no biological readout in the chain, span the full D-range including super-exponential "extraction" (D > 1) — cosmological structures, financial markets, solar flares, NV-diamond T2 relaxation, oceanographic dynamics. These signals carry the S2 form of their underlying physical process, not of a readout.

The asymmetry is the kind of thing H_D4 predicts and the conventional "S2 is just an intrinsic property of data" view does not. If S2 were universally intrinsic, we would expect biological S2 to span the same D-range as non-biological S2. It doesn't.

Why memory specifically

The S2 form was originally a model of memory retention (Ebbinghaus 1885; Wixted 2004). It is no accident that biological memory produces the cleanest S2 fits and the tightest D-distribution — S2 is, by construction, the mathematical shape of biological readout retention. Fitting S2 to non-biological data (solar X-rays, galaxy distributions, BTC prices) is curve-fitting luck on signals that happen to share the stretched-exponential form; fitting S2 to memory is fitting the form to its intended target.

Cross-species test: does D track nervous-system complexity?

Two findings — one predicted, one surprising

1. Mean D correlates with neuron count. Across 10 species spanning 9 orders of magnitude in neuron count, the mean S2 D-value increases systematically from C. elegans (D ≈ 0.17) to humans (D ≈ 0.26). Pearson correlation of log₁₀(neurons) vs mean D: r = +0.69, p = 0.029 — significant. With humans removed, the correlation strengthens to r = +0.73, p = 0.027 — meaning the trend is not driven by the human outlier; it is a real cross-species pattern.

2. D-band width does NOT correlate with neuron count. The within-species D-distribution width (CV, range ratio) is roughly constant across non-human species (CV ≈ 3–8%, range ratio ≈ 1.08–1.24×). Only humans have a notably wider D-band (CV = 18.4%, range ratio = 2.16×). Pearson r(log neurons vs CV) = +0.30, p = 0.40 — not significant.

The monkey-vs-fly problem (and what it tells us about D)

Apparent contradiction: if D-band width tracked IO bandwidth, then a fly and a monkey would have roughly equivalent IO toolkits (both have D-bands around CV ≈ 3–7%). But an ape exhibits dramatically more complex behavior than a fly — tool use, social reasoning, planning. So either H_D4 is wrong, or D-band width is the wrong proxy.

The resolution is that D — the stretch exponent itself — has two distinct meanings in the S2 form, and only one of them tracks IO bandwidth:

  • D measures the SHAPE of the retention function. Lower D = more sub-exponential = slower scrambling. Higher D = closer to exponential (and at D > 1, super-exponential = "extraction"). A fly and a monkey can have similar D (≈ 0.28) because their retention functions have similar shape — both are sub-exponential, both never extract.
  • D-band width (CV) measures CONSISTENCY of the readout across paradigms. A fly has narrow D-band because all its memory paradigms produce D ≈ 0.28. A human has wide D-band because different paradigms (free recall, Stroop, RT distributions, visual perception) produce D = 0.15–0.33. The width is not the IO bandwidth; it's the variability across readout modes.
  • Behavioral complexity tracks neither D nor D-band width directly. It tracks the readout's capacity — channel count, integration time, processing speed, modality diversity, plasticity. These are orthogonal to the S2 retention shape. A fly and a monkey can both have D ≈ 0.28 (similar retention shape) but the monkey has more channels, faster integration, more plasticity — hence more complex behavior. S2 D is the stretch exponent of one retention curve, not a count of channels.

This refines H_D4: the S2 D parameter measures the shape of the biological readout's retention transfer function, not the readout's capacity. Mean D tracks a real biological property (nervous-system complexity, behavior complexity per neuron) — but D-band width tracks consistency across paradigms, which is a different question. The fly-vs-monkey gap in behavior is in capacity, not in retention shape.

An interesting pattern in mean D

Mean D does not strictly track neuron count — honeybees (D = 0.31) have higher mean D than rats (D = 0.24) despite 200× fewer neurons. This is consistent with the well-documented behavioral complexity of bees (waggle dance, multi-modal navigation, social learning) relative to their tiny nervous system: bees' S2 fits look more like vertebrates' than their neuron count would suggest. The hypothesis does not claim to know why; it observes the pattern in the data.

Conversely, C. elegans (302 neurons, D = 0.17) and sea slugs (20K neurons, D = 0.20) sit at the bottom of the mean-D range, consistent with their dramatically simpler behavioral repertoires (mostly habituation and sensitization). The mean D tracks behavioral complexity more faithfully than it tracks raw neuron count. H_D4 predicts the constrained D-range pattern; the cross-species correlation refines where the constraint shows up. The underlying mechanism — why biological readouts produce sub-exponential retention and why mean D tracks nervous-system complexity — is not part of the hypothesis, which stays on the observable side.

Caveats and edge cases

Perception is not memory

Stevens' magnitude-estimation data (n = 13 perceptual modalities) violates the strict pattern. Perceptual D-values span [0.33, 3.50] — including the super-exponential regime for extreme stimuli like electric shock (D = 3.50), force (D = 1.70), warmth (D = 1.60). These outliers don't refute H_D4 — they show that Stevens' power-law magnitude estimation is fitting a different psychometric function (subjective magnitude vs physical intensity) that happens to also fit a stretched-exponential form with different parameters. H_D4 is a hypothesis about the S2 retention form, not about every psychometric function that can be approximated by a stretched exponential.

All biological data is still measured by non-life instruments

Ebbinghaus used a stopwatch. Murre & Dros used a computer scoring system. Stevens used paper records. Strictly speaking, every biological dataset we have is a non-life instrument measuring a biological process. The hypothesis predicts a gap between biological-perception S2 and physical-measurement S2, but we have no pure biological-perception S2 data — no instrument measures "what humans experience" without going through a non-life measurement layer. The test we ran is the closest possible indirect test: we compare S2 of biological-process signals (measured by non-life) vs S2 of non-biological signals (measured by non-life). The biological signal's S2 reflects the biological readout's IO constraint even though the measurement device itself doesn't.

n = 83 is small for biology

Rubin & Wenzel's meta-analysis covers 210 forgetting datasets; we represented them with 50 sample D-values. Fetching the full table from the original paper would give 4× more memory data points. The journal page is Cloudflare-protected from automated access; a manual download would push n from 83 to ~250 and tighten the bootstrap CI further.

What would refute H_D4

H_D4 makes specific falsifiable predictions. Each of the following, if observed, would weaken or refute the hypothesis:

  1. A biological memory dataset with D > 1. Currently 100% of memory D-values are sub-exponential. A single well-fit biological memory S2 with D > 1 (super-exponential) would break the asymmetry that H_D4 predicts. (Note: this would have to be true retention, not a curve that fits S2 only over a narrow regime.)
  2. A non-biological dataset cluster with mean D ≈ 0.33 and CV ≈ 38%. If a domain of non-biological signals (say, all cosmological data) clusters as tightly as biological memory, the "narrow D-range = biological readout" prediction loses discriminative power. The registry's most homogeneous non-biological domain is solar_energy (D ≈ 0.057, CV = 21%) — but it's a tiny cluster (n = 6), and its mean is far from biological (0.057 vs 0.33).
  3. A biological readout type that spans the full D-range. If some other biological readout (e.g., a population of cortical neurons, mood ratings, multi-day EEG) showed D values spanning [0.1, 10] like the non-biological registry, H_D4's "biological readouts produce constrained S2" prediction would be in trouble. We don't have such data yet.
  4. Cross-species mean-D ordering violation. Refined H_D4 predicts that mean D tracks nervous-system complexity (r = +0.69 across 10 species, p = 0.029). A well-sampled species with high behavioral complexity but anomalously low mean D — or vice versa — would weaken this prediction. Currently, the honeybee (high D for its neuron count) and C. elegans (low D for its behavioral simplicity) both fit the prediction; a reversal in either direction with more data would challenge it.
  5. A species with high neuron count but tight D-band. Currently, humans are the only species with a wide D-band (CV = 18.4%); all other species cluster at CV = 3–8%. If a well-sampled non-human species (say, a dolphin or a corvid) showed a wide D-band like humans, this would suggest that D-band width tracks something other than nervous-system complexity — perhaps paradigm diversity in the literature rather than actual readout variability.

Reproducibility

The full statistical analysis (Mann-Whitney, KS, Levene, permutation test on variance, Cohen's d, bootstrap on CV, leave-one-out sensitivity, subdomain breakdown) is implemented in a single Python script. The compiled D-values from psychophysics literature are stored as a JSON data file. All results are saved to a JSON output file.

  • Analysis script: scripts/test_h_d4_comprehensive.py (runnable, scipy-dependent)
  • Biological D-values: compiled in-script from Rubin & Wenzel 1996, Wixted 2004, Stevens 1957–1975, and classic memory studies (Ebbinghaus, Murre & Dros, Wixted & Ebbesen, Hellyer, Anderson & Schooler, Wickelgren, Sikström, etc.)
  • Non-biological D-values: en/tests.json (the 338-test DREAM registry)

Speculative framing

Speculative interpretation. If H_D4 holds up under further testing, the DREAM projection kernel \(K_\lambda\) is recast as a model of the biological readout's transfer function — the empirical object the framework has access to — rather than a model of the inaccessible source domain. The axioms (A1, A2) already state that MM and the kernel are mathematical devices used to represent inaccessible structure; H_D4 sharpens this by adding a falsifiable empirical prediction: the S2 form and its constrained D-range appear in biological readout data, while the same form appears without that constraint in non-biological data.

This says nothing about MM as an object. The 4D world, the kernel, the 10D modeling choice are all (per the axioms) instruments for deriving testable consequences, not descriptions of an independently observable reality. What H_D4 does say: when we fit S2 to a measurement, whether the fit falls in the biological readout cluster (D < 1, CV ≈ 38%) or in the non-biological cluster (any D, CV ≈ 99%) is an observable property of the measurement chain, not of MM. The biological readout's S2 is what it is because biological readouts scramble retention in a constrained way; the non-biological measurement's S2 is whatever it is for that physical process.

The 4D observables we measure — redshifts, X-ray fluxes, memory retention curves — are all on the projection side (the accessible side). H_D4 makes no claim about the hidden domain. It claims that the S2 form on the accessible side carries information about the measurement chain (biological readout vs non-biological measurement) that is observable in the D-distribution. That is the testable content; everything else stays, as the axioms require, in the modeled realm.
