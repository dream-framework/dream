Falsification

Fail gates are expressed strictly in terms of kernel invariants (\(\lambda_q, D_{\mathrm{eff}}\)) and instrument-level observables.

This page defines what would decisively falsify each claim, how to pre-register analyses, and how to interpret nulls. Math is hidden by default — use the global “Math” toggle in the header.

What falsification means in DREAM

  • Invariant-based. Claims are phrased in terms of \(\lambda_q\), \(D_{\mathrm{eff}}\), and directly measured quantities.
  • No post-hoc rescue. Kernel forms are not adjusted to save a failed claim; only pre-declared ranges may be updated.
  • Minimal-Extension Principle. A failed prediction is retained as a limitation or falsification pressure. It is not repaired by introducing a new DREAM-specific mechanism, hidden entity, or free parameter. This is the framework's severe failure criterion: if a major class of phenomena cannot be represented without ad hoc extension, that is evidence against the sufficiency of DREAM.
  • Cross-platform checks. Where feasible, confirm with independent instruments (photonics, atoms, imaging, radio) to rule out artifacts.

Pre-registration protocol

  1. Declare invariants: prior ranges for \(\lambda_q\), \(D_{\mathrm{eff}}\), with rationale.
  2. Map instrument scale: specify how baselines, wavelengths, or coherence times map to \(\lambda\).
  3. Fix analysis windows: linear windows in the transform \(y=\ln(-\ln R)\) vs. \(\ln\lambda\); survey bins for cosmology; λ-grids for lensing.
  4. State fail gates: thresholds for exclusion (CIs, Bayes factors, or equivalent).
  5. Publish pipelines: code, injections, and minimal data for re-analysis.

S2 — Retention law Foundation · guardrailed

Claim: contrast and correlation follow the stretched-exponential law and exhibit a transition at \(\lambda_q\).

\[ R(\lambda)=\exp[-(\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_q})^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}],\quad y(\lambda)=\ln(-\ln R)=D_{\mathrm{eff}}\ln\lambda-D_{\mathrm{eff}}\ln\lambda_q. \]

Decisive null: multiple platforms show no linear window in \(y\) and no shared \(\lambda_q\) within error bars → the retention law fails.

Guardrail falsification: S2 "present" requires passing all 5 formal guardrails (fit quality R² > 0.90, parameter stability CV < 30%, null destruction ratio > 2×, identifiability, AICc). A dataset where S2 fits but fails any guardrail does not count as S2-present. Current survival rate: 84.6% of eligible datasets. The 15.4% failures (trend-dominated economic aggregates, power-law seismic decay) are genuinely non-S2 — denoising does not rescue them.

T7.1 — Multi-Regime S2 Confirmed · 25 datasets · p=3.4×10⁻¹⁸

Claim: S2 is the local law, but the global curve is a piecewise composition of S2 regimes with transitions at reproducible fractional positions; D systematically increases from regime 1 to regime 2.

\[ R(\lambda)\;\approx\; \begin{cases} \exp\!\Big[-\Big(\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{q,1}}\Big)^{D_1}\Big] & \lambda<\lambda^\*\\[4pt] A_2\,\exp\!\Big[-\Big(\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{q,2}}\Big)^{D_2}\Big] & \lambda\ge\lambda^\* \end{cases} \]

Decisive null (T7.1 fails if any)

  • Null 1 fails to control: pure single-S2 synthetic curves show the same rate of strong piecewise improvement as real data (i.e., the piecewise fit is just overfitting noise). Tested: only 20% of single-S2 curves showed strong improvement (vs 100% real), binomial p = 3.4×10⁻¹⁸. Null passes.
  • Positive control fails: pipeline cannot recover known 2-regime t_break in synthetic 2-regime data. Tested: 98% recovery within 0.1 of true t_break=0.3. Positive control passes.
  • Real data fails pattern: piecewise-S2 does NOT strongly beat single-S2 on real curves (ΔAICc < 4 in <50% of cases), OR transition positions do NOT cluster (std > 0.20 in fractional coordinates), OR D does NOT systematically increase (D₂ > D₁ in <50% of cases). Tested: 25/25 (100%) strong improvement; std = 0.169 (cluster); 22/25 (88%) show D₂ > D₁. All three sub-nulls rejected.

Why this is a real test (not overfitting)

T7.1 uses TWO independent controls:

  • NULL 1 (single-S2 synthetic): 60 synthetic single-S2 curves with same n=80, t-range, and noise as real data, run through the IDENTICAL piecewise pipeline. Only 20% showed strong piecewise improvement (vs 100% real). Binomial p = 3.4×10⁻¹⁸. The real signal is NOT a fitting artifact.
  • POSITIVE CONTROL (known 2-regime): 60 synthetic 2-regime S2 curves with true t_break = 0.3, run through the identical pipeline. 98% of fits landed within 0.1 of the true value. The pipeline correctly identifies 2-regime structure when it exists.

Both controls pass. The pipeline is validated. The 25-dataset real result is not overfitting. See Predictions T7.1, Theorems T7.1, and tests registry for the full evidence chain.

2026 Falsification Program — Five New Tests

Through 2026 we ran five tests targeting the strongest form of each DREAM quantitative claim. The results: one foundational claim survived (multidomain stretched-exponential form); several original quantitative claims were falsified; one proposed rescue (snake pit) was rejected; one observational signal (z_depth trend) was traced to standard survey systematics rather than a kernel signature. The framework admitted each falsification without post-hoc repair, per the Minimal-Extension Principle.

Below each card states the claim, the test, the result, and the disposition. See Tests for full registry entries with figures and code pointers.

F1 — Probe-Invariance of D Falsified 2026 D varies 2–10× across 5 probes on same data. Form survives (r²>0.96); invariance claim falsified. Click for details.

Original claim (A3 in its strongest form): S2 is an intrinsic law of MM, so D and λ_q should be invariant across probes applied to the same data.

Test: apply five probes — Gaussian blur, Fourier cutoff, Box (moving average), Wavelet decomposition, PCA truncation — to the same five datasets (SDSS, DESI, Bitcoin, Ethereum, Cognitive reaction times) and extract D and λ_q from each. If S2 is intrinsic, D should match within error.

Result: D varies across probes by factors of 2–10 on every dataset. Examples on SDSS: D = 1.16 (Gaussian), 1.95 (Fourier), 1.31 (Box), 2.09 (Wavelet), 10.0 (PCA-bound). The form \(R(\lambda) = \exp[-(\lambda/\lambda_q)^D]\) fits all five probes (r² > 0.96) — the form survives — but the parameters are probe-dependent.

Disposition: FALSIFIED. "S2 is an intrinsic law of MM" is rejected. The surviving claim is "the stretched-exponential is a probe-dependent coarse-graining regularity" — a weaker but multidomain claim. See Probe-Invariance Test.

F2 — S2 vs P(k)+Filter Mixed — S2 Wins on Cosmology SDSS: S2 r²=0.999 vs P(k)+filter 0.908 (Δr²=+0.09). DESI: +0.14. Financial: P(k)+filter slightly better. Click for details.

Tested claim: does the stretched exponential add information beyond what the power spectrum + filter transfer function already predict?

Test design (three experiments): (1) Synthetic control — generate fields with known P(k), compute the exact analytical R(λ) from \(\int P(k)|H(k,\lambda)|^2 dk / \int P(k) dk\), fit the Weibull to the same R(λ), ask how much information the Weibull loses vs the exact integral. (2) Real data — for each real dataset, compare r² of S2 fit vs r² of exact P(k)+Gaussian prediction. (3) Out-of-sample prediction — split data, fit S2 on half 1, predict R(λ) on half 2; compare to P(k)+filter pipeline.

Results:

  • Exp 1 (synthetic): on six known-spectrum fields, the Weibull loses only \(\Delta r^2 \approx 0.01\) to 0.04 vs the exact integral. The Weibull is a tight summary; little information is lost.
  • Exp 2 (real data): on SDSS the S2 fit gives r² = 0.999 vs the P(k)+Gaussian exact r² = 0.908 — S2 wins by \(\Delta r^2 = +0.092\). On DESI, S2 r² = 0.997 vs P(k)+filter r² = 0.857 — S2 wins by \(\Delta r^2 = +0.140\). On Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Cognitive data, P(k)+filter is competitive or slightly superior where applicable.
  • Exp 3 (out-of-sample): S2 and P(k)+filter perform comparably on financial data; S2 retains its advantage on cosmological data.

Disposition: MIXED. S2 is a useful summary that beats the standard pipeline on cosmological fields by a meaningful margin; the standard pipeline is competitive or superior on financial time series. S2 is not "redundant with P(k)+filter" — it is sometimes the better summary. See S2 vs P(k)+Filter Test.

F3 — Scale-Dependence of D Probe Systematics, Not Law 5 scale measures tested. Only z_depth shows strong real-data trend (slope −0.36/z, r²=0.75). Synthetic L_min effect has sign flip = probe artifact. Click for details.

Tested claim: D varies predictably with a measurable scale property of the probe (L_max, L_min, L_max/L_min, V_eff, or z_depth). A predictable relationship would be the kernel-as-manifestation proposal's first empirical hook.

Test design: two experiments. (1) Synthetic controlled fields — 17 (L_max, L_min) configurations on a 64³ grid with a known P(k) ~ k^-2 power law; compute D from the Gaussian coarse-graining retention curve. (2) Real cosmological data — slice SDSS DR16 LRG and DESI DR1 LRG into 6 redshift shells; compute D, L_max, L_min, V_eff, z_depth per shell. Test slope of D vs each measure in both experiments.

Results:

  • L_max: synthetic NULL (slope ≈ 0, r² = 0.001); real WEAK negative (r² = 0.47).
  • L_min: synthetic STRONG positive (slope = +0.14, r² = 0.96); real WEAK negative (slope = −0.001, r² = 0.57). Sign flip indicates the synthetic effect is a probe transfer function, not a scale law.
  • L_max/L_min (dynamic range): NULL in both.
  • V_eff (volume): synthetic NULL; real WEAK negative.
  • z_depth (redshift): synthetic N/A; real STRONG negative (slope = −0.36/z, r² = 0.75).

Disposition: the only scale measure that shows a strong real-data trend is z_depth. The synthetic L_min effect, while strong, has the opposite sign in real data — confirming it is a probe transfer function. The z_depth trend is the one survivor; see F4 below for its attribution. See Scale-Dependence Test.

F4 — z_depth Trend vs Standard LCDM Survey Systematics, Not Kernel Real slope −0.36/z (r²=0.75); LCDM linear+nonlinear ≈ +0.001 (flat). Opposite signs → z_depth is survey systematics, not kernel. Click for details.

Question: the F3 z_depth trend (D decreases with redshift) is real — but is it the kernel signature predicted by the probe-as-manifestation proposal, or standard cosmological evolution (more small-scale power at higher z) plus survey systematics?

Test design: generate LCDM Gaussian random fields at z = 0.4, 0.5, 0.65, 0.8, 0.95, 1.1 using the Eisenstein-Hu transfer function (linear) and the Smith-Takahashi Halofit (nonlinear correction). Apply the DREAM Gaussian coarse-graining probe at scales 4–100 Mpc/h. Fit D at each z. Compare the predicted D-vs-z slope to the real SDSS+DESI slope (−0.36/z, r² = 0.75).

Results: LCDM predicts slope ≈ +0.001/z (essentially flat, slightly positive) for both linear and nonlinear cases. Real data slope = −0.36/z (strongly negative). The slopes have opposite signs — pure LCDM with a Gaussian probe does not reproduce the trend.

Interpretation: the z_depth signal is not a kernel signature. It is most plausibly a survey-level retention effect — tracer bias evolution (high-z LRGs are more biased), redshift-space distortions (Kaiser + Fingers-of-God), and selection function evolution. Each of these is standard cosmology, not DREAM kernel physics. The probe-as-manifestation proposal does not generate a sign prediction that survives contact with the data.

Disposition: the z_depth trend is real-data retention, not MM retention. The probe-as-manifestation proposal loses its last empirical hook and survives only as a philosophical re-interpretation. See z-Depth vs LCDM Test.

F5 — Snake-Pit Proposal Does Not Resolve Falsifications Multi-S2 field "rescue" — fails F1 (post-hoc), contradicts white-noise cognition match, doesn't predict z_depth sign. Rejected per Minimal-Extension Principle. Click for details.

Proposed rescue: relocate dynamics onto a multi-valued S2 field — "many S2 components at every spacetime point, possibly interacting" — so that the kernel manifests locally rather than globally. Would this resolve the F1–F4 falsifications?

Analysis against each falsification:

  • F1 (probe invariance): different probes would project onto different components of the multi-S2 field, giving variable D — consistent with F1 but only explains the variation we already observed; does not predict it in advance. Post-hoc accommodation.
  • White-noise cognition reproduction: if every point carries its own S2 ensemble, the cognition curve would be a smeared average of many D values. Instead, one D matched to 4 decimal places. A multi-valued field is contradicted by the homogeneity of the cognition-curve match.
  • MM weather null: an uncorrelated snake pit reduces to white noise (already in the null). A correlated snake pit predicts a coherent MM-weather signal that should have been detected. Neither helps.
  • F2–F4: adding S2 components per point does not change probe math, does not change how D varies with survey parameters, does not reproduce the z_depth sign.

Disposition: REJECTED. The snake pit adds degrees of freedom without adding predictive power. It is the wrong direction — the framework should narrow its claims to what the data supports, not broaden them to evade falsification. Per Minimal-Extension Principle, this rescue is not adopted.

F6 — The Pixel is Real Open · Live-tracked DREAM's strongest surviving claim: λq is a structural cutoff of the projection kernel, not an engineering limit. Resolution curve should flatten at SOME scale (DREAM does not predict a specific value for λq). Live tracker scouts arXiv daily. Click for details.

Claim (axiom A6): the micro-macro abyss IS the λq cliff. λq is a structural property of the projection kernel — not an engineering limit. No amount of probe improvement can push λq lower; better probes resolve the cliff more sharply but don't move it. The pixel is real.

Standard-physics prediction: the resolution curve keeps dropping with engineering progress. No structural floor — push isolation harder, the boundary moves further.

DREAM prediction (falsifiable): the published resolution curve flattens (saturates) at the kernel's λq. If the curve keeps dropping with engineering improvements indefinitely, DREAM is falsified. If it flattens at some scale, DREAM is supported — that scale is λq. DREAM does not predict the specific numerical value of λq.

Test status: Open — live tracker running. Today's best optical toolkits resolve at 10⁻¹⁴ m. DREAM does not predict a specific value for λq — it is treated as a measured constant (axiom A4), not a derived one. Daily scout of arXiv for new optical-resolution records.

Updated daily via GitHub Actions. ★ Config space tests: spectral + correlation pixel tests — both saturating, consistent with DREAM's prediction that the kernel is existentially myopic in every configuration space.

Why this is DREAM's strongest surviving claim: the 2026 program discredited D-invariance across probes (F1) — but the pixel claim doesn't require D to be invariant. It requires λq to be a structural cutoff that doesn't move with probe engineering. Those are different claims, and only the first was tested. F6 is alive and testable — and it's the claim that most cleanly distinguishes DREAM from competitors (standard QM, many-worlds, objective collapse, Bohmian — none predict a structural floor).

This follows directly from the pixel claim and is testable: if the pixel is confirmed (structure curve flattens), no singularity is observable in 4D.
