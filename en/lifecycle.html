The Information Lifecycle

Thirteen tests (T19–T31) that turned one question — what does the kernel do to the 4D observable's information content? — into a quantitative lifecycle science: the 4D-projected innovation rate is born, ages, and dies by a Weibull mortality law in every domain we can measure. Validated on solar X-rays, macroeconomics, weather, tides, 15,873 Colombian transformers, 17,939 Canadian transformers, and 10,000 CNC machines. The final test (T31) attempted to extrapolate the aggregate to the pipeline itself; its speculative block was retracted the same day as contradicting the Knowledge Base. Analogy note: the pipeline+faucets analogy was introduced in Test 17 with water = MM/source. T19/T20 then identified "water" with the 4D observable's innovation rate. That elides the projection: what the pipe carries (MM-side $\Phi_A$, unobservable) and what comes out of a faucet (4D-side $\phi_a$, the projected observable) are not the same stuff — the kernel is non-invertible (KB A3). The math of T19–T31 is about $\phi_a$ (the 4D output); only the prose conflated it with $\Phi_A$. The wording below uses "water" for $\Phi_A$ and "the 4D innovation rate" for the measurable quantity T19/T20 actually fit.

Headline results: the retention law $S(W)=\exp(-(W/\lambda)^D)$ is the Weibull survival function — $\lambda$ is a lifetime, $D$ an aging regime (T20), all measured on the 4D observable's innovation rate $\phi_a$ — not on the 10D source field $\Phi_A$ (which is unobservable from 4D per KB A1+A3). The integral $\int S\,dW = \mathbb{E}[T]=\lambda\,\Gamma(1+1/D)$ recovers the physical timescale of each phenomenon, from minutes (solar flares) to decades (price levels) (T21). The same integral on real assets recovers textbook engineering lifetimes with censoring handled properly (T22–T26). The aggregate over 323 measured domains is a valid descriptive statistic of the 4D-D distribution (T31), but its speculative extrapolation to the Meta-Manifold ("MM is draining," "pixel = consequence of finite pipeline") is retracted — see §5.

1 · Decay, not filter DECAY_NOT_FILTER — 4/4

The kernel was described as a filter (a bandwidth limit on what passes from Meta-Manifold to 4D). T20 falsified that framing — but note: "what passes from MM to 4D" is itself a category shortcut. Per KB A2+A3, the kernel does not pass $\Phi_A$ through; it transforms $\Phi_A \to \phi_a$ non-invertibly. What we measure (innovation rate of $\phi_a$) is the projection residue, not a copy of MM content. With that distinction in place: a filter predicts a passband — no loss below a cutoff — and/or an immortal floor. The 4D information substrate shows neither:

  • No floor: immortal-mixture fit collapses to $s_\infty = 0.009 \approx 0$ (GOES).
  • No passband: delayed-onset model collapses to $W_c \to 0$ — loss at every scale.
  • Contrast fails because part of it never decays: $s_\infty = 0.183$, $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=27$ — the persistent background survives all averaging.
  • Controlled mechanism test: add a zero-innovation trend of strength $f$ — contrast's survival tail tracks $f/\sqrt{f^2+1}$ (the immortal variance) while the 4D information retention curve is invariant (depth-range 3.18 ± 0.02 at every $f$).

$S(W)=\exp(-(W/\lambda)^D)$ is the Weibull survival function from reliability engineering. $\lambda$ = characteristic lifetime of the 4D observable's information content (a 4D-measurable quantity). $D$ = aging regime: $D<1$ infant mortality (novelty fades fast when fresh; survivors endure — memory-like), $D\approx1$ memoryless, $D>1$ wear-out (the older information is, the faster it dies). All of this is measured on $\phi_a$, the 4D output of the kernel — not on $\Phi_A$, the 10D source field the pipe carries.

See Tests · T20 for the full prediction set.

2 · The integral: mean information lifetime E[T] = λ·Γ(1+1/D)

Integrating the survival curve — the standard reliability calculation — collapses $(\lambda, D)$ into one number per domain: the mean information lifetime, the average lifespan of one unit of novelty before the kernel consumes it.

$$\int_0^\infty S(W)\,dW \;=\; \mathbb{E}[T] \;=\; \lambda\,\Gamma\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{D}\right)$$

Across 20 datasets, $\lambda$ carries most of the variation ($\rho(\log\lambda,\log \mathbb{E}[T])=0.958$) while $D$ modulates — the integral is a genuinely new dimension, not a re-parameterization of $D$ ($\rho(D,\log\mathbb{E}[T])=-0.275$). And the integral gives back the physical timescale of each phenomenon:

PhenomenonE[T]Physical reading
GOES X-rays≈ 4 minsub-hourly flare variability
NYC precipitation≈ 4 daysone weather system
NOAA tides8–11 h≈ one lunar cycle, then consumed by the next
FRED S&P 500≈ 25 daysone trading regime
FRED CPI (innovation)≈ 76 yrinflation-regime persistence — price surprises outlive careers
FRED GDP, M219–28 yrmacro-aggregate persistence

The tides are the cleanest illustration of the mortality interpretation: a unit of tidal information lives about one tidal cycle, then is consumed by the next. The CPI is the inverse pole: the level is nearly immortal (82% survives 43-year averaging) while the innovation dies 6.3× deeper — and it is the 4D innovation rate (not the MM-side source field) that T19/T20 measure.

Correction of T18 (substrate + sample-scope): the three GMM modes T18 found on the contrast substrate (k=3 best fit on 327 tests, ΔBIC=315 vs k=1) do not organize the info-substrate lifetimes (p=0.13). On the honest info substrate, nearly all aperiodic real-world data is infant-mortality ($D<0.7$); only periodic data sits in wear-out. T18's modes were partly an artifact of measuring static contrast on trending data. Population caveat (per user, 2026-10-30): 327 datasets are a tiny sample of an open-ended population; GMM k=3 is the best fit on this sample (Silverman test refused to confirm multimodality, p=1.0), not a count of "THE" faucet types in the population.

3 · Engineering validation: 44,000 real assets T22–T26

If the integral is a real physical quantity, it must recover known engineering lifetimes. We tested three datasets — two transformer fleets and one CNC-milling dataset — with progressively better statistics: snapshot hazard → Kaplan-Meier with right-censoring → full Weibull MLE per cohort.

Both aging regimes exist in the wild. The Canadian fleet wears out (failures accelerate with age); the Colombian fleet shows infant mortality (survivors endure). Neither is "the" regime — $D$ is data-dependent and physically meaningful, and the constant-hazard shortcut $1/h$ can be off by 2× in either direction depending on the true $D$.

4 · Prediction gain: eliminate the info void, then classify T27–T30

Does the lifecycle model improve prediction? Three architectures, cross-validated:

  • Parallel (T27): Weibull risk as an ML feature. Hybrid beats logistic by +12.9% AUC on the Canadian fleet (0.798 → 0.900) and +23–35% precision@K across the top 1–20% of the risk ranking. Pure Weibull loses on AUC — it carries time and cohort only, no per-unit features.
  • Multidimensional (T28): Weibull AFT, $\log\lambda = \beta_0 + \sum \beta_i x_i$ — time and all covariates simultaneously. Trades AUC (loses to GBM) for what ML cannot give: the global aging regime $D$, physically-signed coefficients (torque −, heat dissipation +), and time projection (when, not just whether).
  • Sequential (T29): user insight — "first estimate novelty decay, then apply ML to surviving novelty; we eliminate info void, noise." (1) AFT baseline → (2) residual = δ − baseline → (3) ML regression on residual → (4) combined. Ties ML on AUC but wins at the top: precision@1% = 0.990 vs RF 0.890 (+11.2%); @2% 0.955 vs 0.745 (+28.2%). The gain concentrates exactly where predictive maintenance budgets live.

T30 surprise: a better baseline makes a worse sequential model. The per-cohort Weibull baseline (standalone AUC 0.545 vs 0.156) loses to the "wrong" global-AFT baseline (sequential AUC 0.683 vs 0.831) — because it removes the manufacturer signal ML needs. The optimal baseline removes the time signal but preserves the feature signal. Bias-variance tradeoff, in residual form.

5 · The pipeline estimate PIPELINE_D_UNSTABLE_TO_IMPUTATION — SPECULATIVE BLOCK RETRACTED

User question: "can we estimate the volume of the pipeline at all? Or at least the pressure? And if we scale it, can we do something really bizarre?"

The aggregate over all faucets is a well-defined statistical operation:

$$S_{\text{pipeline}}(t) \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_q \exp\!\big(-(t/\lambda_q)^{D_q}\big)$$

But T31's original speculative conclusions — that the Meta-Manifold is in infant mortality, that novelty production is declining, and that the pixel is a consequence of the finite pipeline — are RETRACTED. They contradict the Knowledge Base and fail three empirical recheck tests:

  • Temporal category error (vs KB §6.1): the KB states "Time is projected causal order" — time is produced by the projection, not present in MM before it. MM has no time axis to "drain" on. The T31 mixture curve is a cross-sectional average over faucets at one moment of analysis; calling it "draining" conflates cross-sectional shape with longitudinal change. T20 (per-faucet, longitudinal) is real; T31 (cross-faucet, cross-sectional) is not.
  • 10D↔4D composition error (vs KB §1.1): the 4D coordinates $x$ are the image of a non-invertible push-forward from 10D $X$ — they share no axes. Aggregating 323 4D faucet D values and fitting a new Weibull measures a moment of the 4D-D distribution; it does not measure any MM-side quantity. Pipeline-identity test: random repartition of the 323 D values yields $D_{\text{pipeline}} \in [0.37, 1.59]$ — the reported 0.44 sits freely inside the spread of fake pipelines. There is no "the" pipeline D.
  • Imputation sensitivity (recheck refutes the earlier "robust" caveat): with uniform $\lambda$ (the only defensible normalization when per-faucet $\lambda$ is not on disk), $D_{\text{pipeline}}\approx 0.92$ (constant regime); with lognormal-imputed $\lambda$ from 20 engineering cohorts, $D_{\text{pipeline}}\approx 0.44$ (infant mortality). The sign of $D-1$ flips with the imputation.
  • Temporal-anchor test: Spearman of per-faucet $D$ vs measurement date: $\rho=+0.18$, $p=0.0012$ ($n=323$). The trend is significant but in the opposite direction from "draining" — D values measured later are slightly higher. More plausibly selection bias (later tests on more structured/periodic data) than a temporal claim about MM.

What is retracted (contradicts KB): "the Meta-Manifold is draining"; "novelty minimum / the universe runs out of surprise"; "the pixel is a consequence of the finite pipeline." A2 states the kernel has finite resolution; reversing this so the pixel causes the pipeline's finiteness inverts the KB's stated order. What survives: the cross-faucet mixture is a valid descriptive statistic of the 4D-D distribution; the per-faucet Weibull aging-regime findings (T24–T26) and the per-faucet longitudinal finding (T20) that the 4D observable's innovation rate decays in time remain intact. The T31 aggregate adds nothing about MM beyond what the faucet D values already contained.

Recheck script: scripts/t31_recheck_against_manifesto.py. The speculative block was introduced on 2026-10-30 and retracted the same day after the user flagged that MM is atemporal and that 10D axes share none with 4D — both points grounded in KB §1.1 and §6.1.

Retraction context

The speculative T31 block ("MM is draining," "novelty minimum," "pixel = consequence of finite pipeline") was introduced 2026-10-30 and retracted the same day. The retraction is on KB-consistency grounds, not on statistical grounds alone:

  • KB §6.1: "Time is projected causal order" — MM has no time axis before projection; attributing a temporal decay to MM is a category error.
  • KB §1.1: the 4D observable is the image of a non-invertible push-forward from 10D source — the two share no axes. A moment of the 4D-D distribution is not an MM-side quantity.
  • A2 (KB §1.4): the kernel is the finite-resolution entity; saying the pixel follows from the pipeline being finite reverses the KB's stated order.

The empirical recheck (scripts/t31_recheck_against_manifesto.py) confirmed the speculative block was unsupported: the $D<1$ result is not robust to the $\lambda$ imputation (flips sign), random repartitions of the 323 D values give $D_{\text{pipeline}}\in[0.37,1.59]$, and the temporal-anchor test trend is in the wrong direction. The per-faucet findings (T20, T24, T25, T26) and the prediction-gain findings (T27–T30) are unaffected.
