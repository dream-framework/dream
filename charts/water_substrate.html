Test 19 — What is the "water"? Substrate-independence test

User question (b): "Investigate what the 'water' (the substance being processed by faucets) actually IS — information? contrast? something else?" Tested 3 candidate substrates on GOES X-rays (718 samples, 1-min cadence).
VERDICT: INFORMATION PRIMARY
Tested 3 candidate substrates for the kernel's 'water' on GOES X-ray 6-hour data (718 samples, 1-min cadence): (A) S_contrast = std(mavg_W)/std(raw) — current D.R.E.A.M definition; (B) S_info = std(diff(mavg_W))/std(diff(raw)) — INFORMATION / innovation rate; (C) S_power = sqrt(P_low/P_total) — power spectral low-pass. Results: contrast R² = 0.708 (D = 0.087, λ = 0.332 min); information R² = 0.965 (D = 0.386, λ = 1.317 min); power R² = 0.302. INFORMATION gives the cleanest S2 fit (R² = 0.965, decisively better than contrast R² = 0.708). Verdict: INFORMATION_PRIMARY — the kernel's 'water' is INFORMATION (innovation rate, std of differences), NOT contrast (static std of values) as the current D.R.E.A.M definition assumes. This is a significant refinement: the kernel processes the RATE OF NEW INFORMATION per step (the std of differences), not the static contrast. The current S_contrast definition only approximates this — the more fundamental quantity is S_info. For stationary time series the two coincide; for trending or non-stationary data they diverge, and S_info is the correct one. This may explain why some tests in en/tests.json have poor S2 fits — they may be cases where the contrast and information substrates diverge (e.g., strongly trending data where std(values) is dominated by the trend rather than innovation).
