Test 14 — Hologram Hypothesis (Phase Alignment at Common Q*)

If the kernel is a hologram (encodes amplitude AND phase via reference beam), complex couplings should share a common phase at some renormalization scale Q*. Test: complexify α_em, α_W, α_s via 1-loop running + threshold branch cuts; check phase coherence.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
Best alignment at Q* = 1e+04 GeV has max pairwise phase difference of 0.036 rad (2.1°). The null control shows this is MARGINAL — only in the 5th percentile of 1000 random null couplings with similar |Im/Re| ratios (null best = 1.14°, 5th pct = 3.31°, median = 176.51°). The phase alignment is suggestive but not statistically significant. The 1-loop complexification may be too crude to distinguish — 2-loop running + full dispersion relations would give a sharper test. Hologram hypothesis survives but is unsupported. Sub-finding: EM ↔ weak pair aligns EXACTLY (0.0001 rad) at Q ≈ 193 GeV — close to the W-boson threshold. This suggests the EM/weak sectors share analytic structure above the W mass, consistent with electroweak unification. The QCD phase is the outlier at 2° from EM/weak — likely because QCD's β_0 has opposite sign (asymptotic freedom), so its phase grows differently with Q.
