VERDICT: DECAY NOT FILTER
User refinement of Test 19: 'new not needed to be better — with time, more decay.' The information substrate does NOT win because it is better preserved — at every W it retains LESS than contrast. It wins because it is the substrate that DECAYS WITH TIME, without floor and without passband. The retention law S(W) = exp(-(W/λ)^D) is the Weibull survival function: λ is the LIFETIME of information, D the aging regime — the kernel is a mortality process on novelty, not a filter on contrast. (a) Model comparison on GOES (all models raw-RSS optimized, AIC-compared): information is pure decay — M1 beats the immortal-mixture M2 (fitted floor s_∞ = 0.009 ≈ 0) and the delayed-onset filter M3 collapses to immediate onset W_c → 0 (no passband): P1=True. Contrast is NOT pure decay — it needs an immortal floor s_∞ = 0.183 (ΔAIC = 27 in favor of M2): P2=True — the old substrate fails precisely because part of it NEVER decays. Survival analysis: characteristic lifetime λ = 1.75 min, median lifetime 0.80 min. Successive half-life intervals (min): 0.80, 2.70, 4.79, 6.99, 9.28, 11.63 — each halving of surviving novelty takes LONGER than the previous (aging / memory-like fading). Hazard regime: DECREASING_HAZARD (infant mortality: novelty fades fast when fresh; survivors endure). Decay depth -ln S grows ∝ W^D: every doubling of W multiplies it by ×1.39 — 'with time, more decay'. (c) Controlled test (real GOES residuals + zero-innovation trend of strength f, W ≤ 0.1N): contrast's survival tail tracks the immortal-variance prediction f/√(f²+1) to within ±0.01 (0.445 vs 0.447 at f=0.5; 0.985 vs 0.992 at f=8) while its decay-depth range collapses 2.07 → 0.008 — the substrate stops decaying as immortal structure grows. The info-substrate retention curve is INVARIANT: decay-depth range 3.18 ± 0.02, same D and lifetime at every f (P3=True). (d) Real trending data (FRED CPI, monthly, 956 months): the price LEVEL is nearly immortal — even a 43-year moving average retains 82% of its std — while innovation dies 6.3× deeper (1.27 vs 0.20 decay-depth), following the Weibull decay law (lifetime λ ≈ 132 months, D = 0.32, tail 0.23 vs contrast 0.82). Honest footnote: the CPI info curve retains a 0.17 floor — decade-scale inflation-regime variation, itself a slow component of the innovation RATE, still 4× smaller than the level's survival — P4=True.
